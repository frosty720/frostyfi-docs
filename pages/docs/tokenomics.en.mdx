# $FROST Tokenomics

> **$FROST** is FrostyFi's protocol token — earned by paying customers, never presold. Subscribe and your payment mints you FROST through the same bond machinery everyone else uses. Lock it for **PERMA** and earn a share of protocol revenue in USDC.

## FROST and PERMA

Two things, one purpose:

| | **FROST** | **PERMA** |
|---|---|---|
| What | The liquid ERC-20 token | Your **locked FROST position** — soulbound, non-transferable |
| How you get it | Earn it by paying for FrostyFi, or bond for it | Lock FROST for a chosen term (1 week – 4 years) |
| Grants | — | Vote weight + a share of protocol revenue, paid in **USDC** |
| Tradeable | Yes | No — it is the lock position itself, bound to your address |

PERMA is not a token you hold or trade — it is your locked FROST position, recorded on-chain and bound to your address. Vote weight scales with **amount × remaining time** and decays linearly, exactly like Aerodrome's veAERO. It is **soulbound** so nobody can buy or sell your votes or your yield claim — they belong to whoever locked their own FROST.

**Locking is escrow, not spending.** When your term ends you unlock and **your FROST returns to you**; the PERMA position simply ends. The name is literal — lock FROST and it stays frozen as PERMA until you thaw it.

## Supply — allowance-bounded, mint-on-claim

FROST has no arbitrary supply number to memorize. Instead, supply is controlled the way a budget is:

- **Every contract that can mint holds an on-chain `mintAllowance`** — a budget it can never exceed, spent down with every mint. The bond machinery, the subscription rewards, the dev share: each mints only within its granted budget, and the budgets are public state anyone can read.
- **Tokens are minted only when claimed.** Rewards vest first and mint on redemption, so circulating supply reflects what participants actually claimed — nothing sits pre-minted in a treasury wallet waiting to be dumped.
- **No pre-mine, no presale, no VC allocation.** At launch the supply starts at zero and grows only through the mechanisms on this page.

This is a deliberately honest framing: a "hard cap" that governance can amend is theater, while a per-module allowance is enforced in bytecode on every mint. What the owner can grant, the chain shows.

## How you earn FROST

Only **paying subscribers** earn FROST. The free tier earns nothing — there is no farming surface.

> **Subscribe, and your payment is converted to FROST at the live bond price — the same price a bond buyer pays at that moment — granted as a vesting position.**

When you pay for a subscription, the rewards contract reads the current bond price and grants you a FROST bond worth **100% of what you paid** (a governance dial, hard-capped in bytecode at 200%). It vests over days like any bond, then you redeem and the FROST mints to you. No tiers, no tenure multipliers, no separate emission schedule to game — Enterprise earns ~4× Pro because it pays ~4×. A month you don't pay is a month you earn nothing.

Because the reward is priced by the bond market, earning stays fair at any market price: you always receive the same deal a stranger bonding USDC would get, and the same anti-manipulation machinery (debt-based pricing, circuit breaker) protects both.

## The dev share — earned when you earn

FrostyFi is a bootstrapped, sole-operator project — no VC, no presale, and no founder pre-mine.

> **The developer's share is a 10% co-mint: whenever a participant redeems FROST, an additional 10% of that amount mints to the dev wallet. No redemptions, no dev tokens.**

The rate is a governance dial hard-capped in bytecode at 20%. The dev share is structurally downstream of everyone else's: it fires only when customers and bonders actually claim, so the developer's upside is coupled to the protocol being used — and its failure mode ("protocol fails, dev gets ~nothing") is identical to every other participant's.

## Where each dollar goes

Every product payment flows through an on-chain revenue splitter:

| Bucket | Share (subscription revenue) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Referrals | up to 10% (6 / 2.5 / 1.5 across 3 levels) | Paid to the referrer chain in USDC; levels without a referrer roll into POL |
| Operating | 16% | Infrastructure and model costs |
| Dev | 37% | The operator's revenue share |
| DAO / lockers | 7% | Feeds the weekly USDC distribution to PERMA lockers |
| **POL** | **the remainder (~30–40%)** | Buys FROST and pairs it into protocol-owned liquidity |

The splitter is deployed, verifiable, and each bucket lands at a fixed public address on every payment.

## Real yield — lock for USDC

**Holding FROST alone earns nothing.** You have to lock it into a PERMA position to earn — liquid holders get price exposure, not yield.

Lockers are paid in **USDC, on a weekly epoch**: the lockers' revenue share accumulates in an on-chain distributor, each week's pot is credited to that week's total lock weight, and you claim your pro-rata share once the week completes. Your share is your PERMA weight — larger and longer locks earn more, decaying as the lock ages.

<Callout type="info">
Lockers are never paid in FROST. The one hard rule carried through the whole design: **no reward is ever paid in a token whose only use is selling it.**
</Callout>

## Bonds — how the liquidity gets deep

Revenue builds protocol-owned liquidity steadily; bonds build it *fast*. Anyone can deposit into an open bond market — **USDC, WETH, or a Uniswap V3 LP position** — and receive FROST vesting over about 7 days.

- **Priced by debt, not by the pool.** The bond price rises as outstanding bond debt grows and eases as it's repaid — an Olympus-style debt ratio with a hard floor. The pool price never touches the payout, so bending the market can't mint extra FROST.
- **Circuit-breakered.** Issuance per window is capped in the contract; a demand spike slows the machine instead of draining it.
- **Funding, not revenue.** Bond principal goes to the treasury and POL — it never counts as product revenue and never touches the reward path.

The POL bucket does exactly what it says: the vault swaps half its USDC for FROST and mints a real, on-chain Uniswap V3 position owned by the protocol. The position, its size, and its fees are all public — see **[POL](/docs/pol)**.

## Governance — honest about who holds the keys

PERMA is the vote. Proposals, voting, queuing, and execution run through a standard OpenZeppelin Governor wired to lock weight — the full cycle has been executed on testnet, including an on-chain parameter change.

In the current phase, governance is **signaling-first**: fund- and mint-touching contracts stay under the operator's multisig, and passed proposals are actioned by the team. This is deliberate and disclosed — while the community is small, a whale buying a majority of a thin token should not be able to vote themselves the treasury. Control migrates outward in stages as lock distribution makes capture economically silly.
